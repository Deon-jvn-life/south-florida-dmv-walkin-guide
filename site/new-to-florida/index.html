New to Florida: transferring an out-of-state or foreign driver license

In short. Deadline, documents, exam rules, and fees for transferring an out-of-state or foreign license in Broward, Miami-Dade, or Palm Beach.

For people who have just moved to Broward, Miami-Dade, or Palm Beach and already hold a driver license from another U.S. state or from another country. The rules below come from the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV), the 2026 Florida Statutes, the Official Florida Driver License Handbook (revised August 2023), and the county tax collector sites. Where those sources do not match, this page says so. No wait times or tips from social media are included.

A first Florida license is issued in person. Document lists are on what to bring, and on the FLHSMV checklists for a U.S. citizen, an immigrant, a non-immigrant, and a Canadian [source].

When a Florida license is required

FLHSMV's new-resident page says you must get a valid Florida driver license within 30 days of establishing residency to drive on Florida roads. It says you may be considered a resident when you start work or a trade in Florida, enroll your children in a Florida public school, register to vote, file for a homestead exemption, or live in Florida for more than six consecutive months [source]. The visiting-Florida questions page uses the same 30-day sentence and the same five triggers [source].

The statute reads differently. Section 322.01 defines a resident as a person whose principal place of domicile has been in Florida for more than six consecutive months, who has registered to vote, who has made a statement of domicile under section 222.17, or who has filed for a homestead tax exemption. Employment and public school enrollment are not in that definition [source]. Section 322.031 is the 30-day rule, and it applies to a nonresident, other than a migrant or seasonal farm worker, who accepts employment or engages in a trade, profession, or occupation in Florida, or who enters children in a Florida public school. That person must get a Florida driver license within 30 days after the employment or education begins, if he or she drives on Florida highways. A spouse or dependent child must do the same within those 30 days before driving [source].

The 30-day clock is not stated the same way. FLHSMV's new-resident and visiting pages start the 30 days when residency is established, and they treat a job, public school, voter registration, homestead, or six months in Florida as residency. Section 322.031 starts the 30 days when a nonresident begins work or public school and drives here. Section 322.01 does not call employment or school enrollment residency. The August 2023 handbook follows the statute on this split: a resident must have a Florida license to drive, and a nonresident who takes a job or enrolls a child has 30 days in order to drive. The handbook also says it is not a legal authority and does not reflect legislative changes after August 2023 [source, source, source, source, source].

Section 322.031 has exemptions. An active-duty member of the United States Armed Forces in Florida, and a spouse or dependent living with that member, do not have to get a Florida license because of a job or public school if the person has a valid military identification card and either a valid driver license or learner permit from another state, or a valid military driving permit. A nonresident who lives in another state, commutes into Florida for work, and holds a valid license from that state does not need a Florida license only because of the job. A nonresident full-time student at a college or university is exempt during that enrollment. A nonresident in Florida for up to six months in a work-study program is also exempt for that program, when the academic credit comes from a college whose credits or degrees are accepted by at least three accredited institutions. Those student exemptions require a valid license from another state [source]. The handbook states the military, commuter, and college exemptions in shorter form, and it also lists a U.S. government employee on official business and a 60-day exemption for some contract workers [source].

Once you are a resident, section 322.03 says that, except as the chapter allows, you may not drive on a highway in Florida without a valid Florida driver license [source].

If you are still a nonresident and section 322.031 does not apply to you, section 322.04 lets you drive without a Florida license while you have a valid noncommercial license from your home state or country with you. The age cutoff is 16 for a vehicle that needs a Class E license in Florida, and 18 for another noncommercial vehicle. The home license must not be invalid under section 322.033. This exemption does not apply to a person section 322.031 covers [source, source]. The handbook instead says a nonresident from another country needs an International Driving Permit plus a valid license from that country. Section 322.04 does not mention an International Driving Permit [source, source].

Palm Beach County's transfer page states a narrower deadline: a Florida license within 30 days of accepting employment, engaging in a trade, profession, or occupation, or enrolling a child in public school. That page does not mention voter registration, homestead, or six months of residence [source].

Transferring a license from another U.S. state

Apply in person at an office that issues driver licenses. FLHSMV says you will take a vision test, and that you may be eligible for a license without a written test or a road test [source]. Its document questions page says a valid out-of-state license is accepted in exchange for the written and driving exams, that every out-of-state driver takes the vision test, and that other testing may be required if there is a reason to think the person cannot drive safely. That answer also says an out-of-state license is not accepted as primary identification [source].

Section 322.12 says every applicant for an original license is to pass an examination, and that the department may waive the knowledge, endorsement, and skills tests for an otherwise qualified applicant who surrenders a valid license from another state, a province of Canada, or the United States Armed Forces and who applies for an equal or lesser class. The Class E examination in that section still includes an eyesight test and a hearing test. Those two tests are not in the waiver sentence [source, source]. The questions page and the handbook tell transferring drivers to expect a vision test. They do not say whether the hearing test is given on a transfer [source, source].

REAL ID documents for a first Florida card. The REAL ID Act requires original documents for identity, a Social Security number (SSN), and residential address the first time the credential is issued in an office. Bring them for a first Florida license or ID card, if your current card has no star, or after a legal name change. After they are on file, bring them again only if your information changes [source, source].

Identity

For a U.S. citizen, bring one original primary document: a U.S. birth certificate (a Puerto Rico certificate must be issued after July 1, 2010, and a hospital certificate is not accepted), a valid U.S. passport or passport card, a Consular Report of Birth Abroad, a Certificate of Naturalization (N-550 or N-570), or a Certificate of Citizenship (N-560 or N-561). If the name differs, bring the marriage certificates, court orders, or divorce decrees that link the names, unless your valid U.S. passport shows your current name [source].

The questions page says an out-of-state driver license is not primary identification. Section 322.08 lists, as one acceptable proof of identity, a driver license record or identification card record from another jurisdiction that complies with the REAL ID Act and that was issued only after a similar identity document. The plastic card and that record are not described the same way. Bring a primary document from the checklist for your status, and bring the out-of-state license as well [source, source].

Social Security number

Bring one original with your complete name and complete SSN: a Social Security card in your current name, a W-2 that is not handwritten, a pay stub, an SSA-1099, or any 1099 that is not handwritten. The name on the SSN must match the name that will be on the license. After a name change with the Social Security Administration, the citizen checklist says to wait 24 to 48 hours [source]. U.S. citizens and immigrants must have proof of an SSN. A non-immigrant must provide that proof as well, unless the immigration status does not authorize work in the United States. A non-immigrant who presents work-authorized documents but is not authorized to work must bring a Social Security Administration letter of ineligibility, and the reason on that letter cannot be a failure to bring documents [source]. The non-immigrant checklist says a non-immigrant in the country without a work visa does not have to bring the letter that says an SSN was never issued. The two pages do not explain how those rules fit together [source, source].

Two proofs of a Florida residential address

Bring two different documents that show your Florida residential address. Your current license or ID card does not count. Printouts and faxes are accepted. An out-of-state or U.S. territory address is not accepted for a Class E license or an ID card [source]. The citizen checklist includes a deed, mortgage, mortgage statement, or lease; a Florida voter registration card; a Florida vehicle registration or title; and, dated within the last 60 days, items such as a utility bill, an insurance bill, a pay stub, a bank statement, or government mail. If nothing is in your name, the person you live with can complete a Certification of Address (HSMV 71120) with two proofs of their own, signed in front of an examiner or a notary. A commercial learner's permit or commercial driver license (CDL) applicant cannot use that form [source]. Full lists: what to bring.

If you cannot produce the documents, the visiting page says you may be issued a 60-day temporary driving permit when the out-of-state license is valid or has been expired for 60 days or less [source]. That permit is separate from the paper permit for non-U.S. citizens described below.

The handbook says a license from another state or a U.S. territory must be surrendered when the Florida license is issued. Section 322.03 says a person may not have more than one valid driver license at any time [source, source].

A license from another country

Section 322.12 says the department shall examine every applicant, including a person licensed in another state or country, except as the chapter provides. The waiver in that section names only another U.S. state, a province of Canada, or the United States Armed Forces, and only when that license is surrendered and the Florida license is an equal or lesser class. France, Germany, Taiwan, and South Korea are not named [source].

The August 2023 handbook says the Class E knowledge exam and the driving skills exam are waived for a valid license from a U.S. state, territory, or possession; the U.S. military; Canada; France; Germany (driving skills test only); the Republic of China (Taiwan); or South Korea. A vision exam is still required. It says state and U.S. territory licenses must be surrendered. It says a Canadian driver can have the exams waived by surrendering the Canadian license, and that keeping the Canadian license means the Class E exams are required [source].

Palm Beach County says it will waive the Class E knowledge and skills exams for a license from any U.S. state; a U.S. territory or possession, including the Panama Canal Zone if issued before January 1, 2000; Canada; Germany (skills exam only); France; the Republic of China (Taiwan); the Republic of Korea (South Korea); or the United States Armed Forces, whether the military license is for on-base or off-base driving. Its Canada note matches the handbook. Palm Beach also requires the REAL ID documents [source].

Foreign exam waivers conflict. Section 322.12 does not authorize a waiver for France, Germany, Taiwan, South Korea, or a U.S. territory. The 2023 handbook and the Palm Beach transfer page do. The handbook says it is not itself the law and that it stops at its August 2023 revision. Confirm a waiver with the office before you rely on it. A country that is on neither the statute's waiver nor the handbook's list is not described as exempt by either source [source, source, source].

If you have never been issued a driver license in any state or country, FLHSMV requires a Traffic Law and Substance Abuse Education (TLSAE) course before you apply. A license already issued in another country means that sentence does not apply [source].

The Class E knowledge exam is 50 questions. A passing score is 40 correct, which is 80 percent. FLHSMV says that exam is available in English only [source]. After a failed first knowledge test, each later attempt is $10. After a failed first skills test, each later attempt is $20. When the tax collector gives the retest, the tax collector keeps that fee, less the general revenue service charge [source, source].

The Broward County Tax Collector says that, effective February 6, 2026, all driver license knowledge and driving exams in Florida are given in English only, and that translation devices, interpreters, and other language assistance are not allowed on the written test or the road test [source]. FLHSMV's Broward locations page still says a knowledge exam requested with an interpreter is by appointment only and limited to one attempt per day [source]. The FLHSMV knowledge-exam page says the exam is available in English only and does not mention interpreters or the road test [source].

Canadian citizens use the Canadian checklist: an original valid Canadian passport or, if that passport is not presented, a United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) document showing legal presence, plus SSN proof and two Florida address documents. A Canadian citizen classified as a non-immigrant is not eligible for a commercial learner's permit or a CDL [source].

Refugees and other lawfully present people

Section 322.033 says the department may not issue a driver license or identification card to a person who is an unauthorized alien or an undocumented immigrant. Palm Beach states the same limit for its office [source, source]. A class of out-of-state license issued only to people who could not prove lawful presence is invalid in Florida and does not authorize driving here. FLHSMV keeps the list on its Senate Bill 1718 page and says the list can change [source, source, source].

Use the checklist for the status FLHSMV assigns. There is not one list for every non-citizen.

Immigrant checklist. One original: a valid alien registration receipt card (green card, Form I-551); an I-551 stamp in a passport or on Form I-94; an immigration judge's order granting asylum, with the A-number and a valid passport; an I-797 granting asylum, with the A-number and a valid passport; or an I-797 or other USCIS form, with the A-number, stating that a refugee application is approved, plus a valid passport. Then one SSN proof and two Florida address proofs. A legal name change has to be on the USCIS documents [source].
Non-immigrant checklist. One original: a valid employment authorization card (Form I-688B or I-766), or unexpired proof of non-immigrant classification on Form I-94 with the supporting papers. An I-94 must come with a valid passport, a U.S. visa, and an entry stamp. F-1 and M-1 status also need Form I-20. J-1 and J-2 need Form DS-2019. The page says refugee, asylee, and parolee classifications need more documents, and it lists Form I-571 (refugee travel document) with a valid passport, Form I-512 (parole letter) with a valid passport, or an immigration judge's order granting asylum or cancellation of removal with a valid passport [source].

Approved refugee status is on the immigrant checklist. Refugee, asylee, and parolee papers are also on the non-immigrant checklist. Bring the USCIS documents you have, and follow the checklist that matches the status on those documents [source, source].

How long the Florida credential lasts depends on which document was used, and the sources do not draw that line in the same place.

  • Section 322.08 says a driver license or temporary permit issued from an employment authorization card, or from the proof of nonimmigrant classification listed in that section, is valid for a period that does not go past the expiration date of the document presented and does not go past one year [source].
  • The questions page says a Class E license for a citizen or an immigrant is valid for eight years under age 80 and for six years over age 79. It says a non-immigrant credential is issued for the time USCIS approved, not longer than one year. The Class E renewal fee it states for that shorter license is still $48 [source].
  • The non-immigrant checklist says the immigration documents must be valid for more than 60 days from the date of issuance. A non-U.S. citizen applying for an original driver license receives a 60-day temporary paper permit with no photo, plus a receipt. After identity and legal status are verified, the card is mailed within 60 days and is issued for the period on the USCIS document, not longer than one year. If status or identity is not verified, a denial letter is mailed [source].
  • Broward says immigration documents must generally be valid for more than 30 days, and that a Florida license or ID card generally expires with the authorized stay and may be limited to one year in some cases [source]. Thirty days does not match FLHSMV's 60 days. Broward's "generally" is also wider than section 322.08, which attaches the one-year cap to an employment authorization card or to proof of nonimmigrant classification, not to a green card [source, source].

Palm Beach's driver license page says a license or ID card lasts eight years at age 79 and under, and six years at age 80 and older. That sentence does not mention the shorter non-immigrant period [source, source, source].

Fees

Section 322.21 sets an original Class E license at $48, an original or renewal commercial driver license at $75, each endorsement at $7, an original, renewal, or replacement identification card at $25, and a replacement driver license at $25. A renewal made within 12 months after expiration adds a $15 delinquent fee [source]. The FLHSMV fee schedule publishes those amounts and says an additional $6.25 service fee may apply when a tax collector office issues the credential. The new-resident page states the same $48, $75, $7, and $25 figures and says most tax collector offices charge the $6.25 [source, source].

Section 322.135 requires that $6.25 on top of the other driver license fees for services the tax collector provides. It is charged no more than once per customer during one visit. It is not charged to a veteran who presents a DD Form 214, a veteran health identification card, a veteran identification card issued under the Veterans Identification Card Act of 2015, or another form the Department of Veterans' Affairs specifies [source]. The fee schedule says a veteran who provides, or has previously provided, evidence of veteran status is not charged the $6.25. "Previously provided" is wider than the statute's requirement to present the document [source, source].

Palm Beach publishes combined totals: Class E original or renewal, if not expired, $54.25; a CDL at $81.25 plus $7 per endorsement; a state identification card at $31.25; a replacement at $31.25; and $15 extra when the license or ID card is expired. $54.25, $81.25, and $31.25 are the state fee plus $6.25. Palm Beach also lists no fee to replace a stolen license or ID card when its required documentation is provided, and no fee for an ID card for a homeless person [source]. The statute already removes the identification card fee for a homeless applicant who presents evidence that meets the statutory definition [source]. The FLHSMV fee schedule lists a $25 replacement and does not list a no-fee stolen replacement [source].

On the FLHSMV Broward locations page, an asterisk marks a tax collector-sponsored office, and the page says a tax collector service fee is added there. The driver license offices without an asterisk are described as state-sponsored [source].

Broward, Miami-Dade, and Palm Beach offices

Miami-Dade County's tax collector says it issues driver licenses and identification cards for county residents. It says the offices it has taken over take an appointment or a walk-in during operating hours, that it has taken over eight offices, and that it does not name them. An appointment booked online has priority [source]. See Miami-Dade offices and walk-in vs appointment.

Palm Beach County's tax collector issues REAL ID driver licenses and state ID cards as an agent for FLHSMV. A person moving there from another state or country must make a reservation for in-person service [source, source]. See Palm Beach offices and walk-in vs appointment.

Broward is split. On the FLHSMV Broward page, Lauderdale Lakes is the tax collector-sponsored driver license office. Margate, Sunrise, Pembroke Pines, and Pompano Citi Centre are listed as state-sponsored driver license offices, and those rows say appointments [source]. The Broward County Tax Collector also publishes its own driver license document rules [source]. See Broward offices and walk-in vs appointment.

FLHSMV's Broward page tells a new resident to get the Florida driver license first, then automobile insurance from a company licensed to do business in Florida, then the title, registration, and license plate [source]. The new-resident page says that Florida insurance is required in order to title and register a vehicle, vessel, or mobile home within 10 days of establishing residency [source].
